Global Genocides and Atrocities Overview

Scale of Global Atrocities

This treemap documents genocides and atrocities committed by 10 countries across multiple continents, representing some of the most severe crimes against humanity in modern history. The visualization shows:

  • Massive Death Tolls: Combined casualties exceeding 100 million people
  • Ongoing Crises: Several countries continue committing atrocities today
  • Systematic Patterns: Recurring use of genocide, ethnic cleansing, and mass violence
  • Global Impact: Regional destabilization and refugee crises affecting millions

Major Genocides by Region

Asia-Pacific

China (Great Leap Forward: 15-55M deaths, Uyghur genocide), Cambodia (Khmer Rouge: 1.5-3M), Myanmar (Rohingya genocide), North Korea (prison camps, famines)

Europe & Former USSR

Russia (Ukraine war: 50K+ deaths, Chechnya, Soviet invasions), ongoing territorial aggression and war crimes

Middle East

Israel (Gaza operations: 54K-335K deaths), Iran (proxy wars, domestic suppression), Saudi Arabia (Yemen intervention: 250K+ deaths)

Africa

Sudan (Darfur genocide: 98K-500K deaths), Rwanda (1994 genocide: 800K-1M deaths, DRC massacres)

Ongoing Atrocities (2023-Present)

Several countries continue committing atrocities with impunity:

  • Russia: Ukraine invasion with mass killings, child deportations, and forced "Russification"
  • Israel: Gaza operations labeled genocide by UN experts due to civilian targeting
  • China: Continued persecution of Uyghurs with mass detention and cultural erasure
  • Myanmar: Post-coup civil war exacerbating persecution of minorities
  • Sudan: Civil war since 2023 with mass rapes and ethnic cleansing

Patterns of Impunity

Despite extensive documentation, most perpetrators avoid accountability through:

  • UN Security Council Vetoes: Permanent members blocking investigations and sanctions
  • State Sovereignty Claims: Rejecting international jurisdiction and oversight
  • Geopolitical Protection: Allies providing diplomatic and military cover
  • Information Warfare: Denying, minimizing, or justifying atrocities

Documentation Sources

This visualization is compiled from credible sources including:

  • Human Rights Organizations: Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International
  • United Nations: UN reports, fact-finding missions, and expert panels
  • International Courts: ICC indictments and international tribunal findings
  • Academic Research: Peer-reviewed studies and historical documentation
  • Investigative Journalism: Credible media investigations and witness testimonies
